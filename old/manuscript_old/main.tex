\begin{abstract}
The portfolio optimization problem asks for the best allocation among a set of assets, balancing expected return against risk under given constraints. In this work, we study a quantum walk optimization algorithm for constrained binary portfolio selection, based on restricting the dynamics to the subspace of feasible solutions. Each asset is represented by a binary variable, and the budget constraint fixes the Hamming weight of the admissible bit strings. We compare quantum-walk optimization algorithm (QWOA) mixers defined by
different graphs on the feasible configurations, including the complete graph,
the Johnson graph, and cost-weighted Johnson graphs. We show that complete-graph QWOA is equivalent to the usual quantum approximate optimization algorithm (QAOA) with a Grover mixer when the initial state is prepared as a Dicke state. Using benchmarks over several market windows, cardinalities, circuit depths, and optimizer seeds, we find that Johnson-type mixers outperform the complete/Grover mixer and a penalty-based QAOA baseline in the tested instances.

\end{abstract}
