\begin{abstract}
The quantum approximate optimization algorithm (QAOA) and the quantum walk
optimization algorithm (QWOA) are usually presented as separate
constructions, the first through Pauli mixers and the second through a graph
on the feasible set. The two coincide in particular cases, but the
correspondence has been established instance by instance. We state it in
general, for an arbitrary feasibility-preserving mixer, and use it to identify
the mixers in current use as walks on named graphs. The complete-graph $XY$
mixer with a Dicke initial state is exactly a walk on the Johnson graph
$J(n,k)$, with no residual phase and no rescaling. The Grover mixer and the
complete-graph QWOA mixer are the same operator, up to a global phase and a
rescaling of the mixing parameter. The ring $XY$ mixer walks on a graph that
is not regular, whose vertex degree we give in closed form; we classify the
connectivity graphs whose mixer is regular, for $2\le k\le n-2$ only the
complete graph, except at $n=2k$, where the star and its complement also
qualify, and we show that $J(n,k)$ is the sparsest graph on the feasible set
invariant under permutations of the assets at the sizes considered here. The
Johnson graph and the complete graph are the sparse and dense endpoints of a
family of regular, vertex-transitive graphs obtained from the distance classes
of the Johnson scheme. Benchmarking fixed-cardinality portfolio selection
across that family, we find the Johnson mixer on par with or ahead of the
denser members at $(12,3)$ and $(16,4)$ at every depth tested, and behind them
by a factor of about $1.5$ at $(20,5)$ on six instances, while the complete
graph is worse than all of them on every metric at the depths reachable on
near-term hardware. It is also the more expensive of the two to realise, by a
factor of eight in \textsc{cnot} count and thirty to sixty in circuit depth,
against a single Trotter step of the $XY$ mixer.
\end{abstract}

\reviewmajor[-- Optimizer reliability]{The reported median factor $1.1$--$1.8$ by which exhaustive $p=1$ search can beat the optimizer is large enough that depth rankings may partly measure optimizer difficulty. \reviewexperiment[-- Optimizer calibration]{Use $p=1$ as a gold standard. Compare COBYLA with differential evolution or CMA-ES and another multistart local method; report optimization gap by mixer. Repeat a representative subset at $p=2$--$4$ with larger restart budgets and test ranking stability.}
\reviewexperiment[-- $q$ and screening robustness]{Document the 30-instance $q=0.75$ pilot and any overlap with evaluation data. Add a $p=1$ sweep over several $q$ values and report an unscreened benchmark in addition to the hard-instance conditioned sample.}
\reviewreplace{``At $(20,5)$ ... the ordering inverts.''}{``At $(20,5)$, within the six-instance subset and for $p\le4$, the observed ordering among the non-complete mixers differs from that at $(16,4)$. Given the small sample and overlapping uncertainty intervals, this is exploratory and does not establish a size-dependent reversal.''}
\reviewmajor[-- Statistics]{With up to 31 graphs, uncorrected pairwise sign tests should not support confirmatory claims. Prefer paired effect sizes/bootstrap intervals and use a stated correction such as Holm--Bonferroni for formal multiple tests.}
\reviewexperiment[-- Spectral/DLA mechanism]{For tractable sizes compute or bound $\dim\operatorname{Lie}\{iH_C,iA_T\}$ for representative mixers. At minimum report spectral degeneracies, gaps, and numbers of distinct eigenvalues for every $A_T$, and relate them to $P_*$ and $\Delta_{\rm norm}$.}
\reviewexperiment[-- Geometry versus degree]{Relate performance to descriptors beyond degree: distinct eigenvalues, spectral gap, diameter where meaningful, distance-class composition, and mixing/localization diagnostics.}
\reviewreplace{Claims that $K_{\F}$ is intrinsically eight times more expensive in CNOTs and thirty-to-sixty times deeper than Johnson.}{``For the compiled circuits considered here, an exact implementation of $K_{\F}$ uses substantially more gates and depth than one first-order Trotter step of the Johnson $XY$ mixer. Since the latter is approximate, these raw factors are not an equal-accuracy resource comparison.''}
\reviewexperiment[-- Equal-accuracy resources]{Compare exact $U_{XY}(\beta)$ with product formulas for $r=1,2,4,8,\ldots$ at representative optimized angles. Report state infidelity or changes in final $P_*$ and energy and compare CNOT/depth at matched accuracy. Add finite-shot/noise/routing experiments if near-term claims are retained.}
